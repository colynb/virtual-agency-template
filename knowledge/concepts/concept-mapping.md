# Concept Mapping

## Summary

Concept mapping is a visual technique for organizing and representing knowledge. Concepts are shown as nodes (often boxes or circles) connected by labeled arrows that describe the relationship between them.

## Detail

Developed by Joseph Novak in the 1970s, concept maps differ from mind maps in a key way: they emphasize **cross-links** between different branches, making them better suited for showing how ideas in different domains connect. Mind maps are hierarchical (tree-shaped); concept maps are networked (graph-shaped).

Effective concept maps:
- Place the most general concepts at the top or center
- Use clear, specific linking phrases on edges ("causes", "requires", "is an example of")
- Include cross-links that reveal non-obvious connections
- Can be read as propositions: "Node A — linking phrase — Node B" forms a meaningful sentence

## Connections

Concept mapping is the **visual layer** on top of a **knowledge graph** — the graph provides the data structure, and the concept map provides the human-readable visualization. Both feed into **personal knowledge management** as tools for organizing what you know.

## Open Questions

- What's the right level of detail for a concept map? Too sparse loses value, too dense becomes unreadable.
- Should layout be manual (user-controlled) or algorithmic (force-directed)?
